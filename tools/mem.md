# Mem

Mem is the notes app for people who hate folders. The premise is simple: you write things down, Mem indexes and connects them, and the AI surfaces the right note at the right time. The "self organising" pitch sounds gimmicky until you've used a tagged + foldered system long enough to resent it.

## What it actually is

A notes app (web, macOS, iOS) with AI baked in across the surface. Features include AI generated tags, semantic search, "Mem It" for sending content from anywhere, and Mem X — an assistant that drafts and answers questions from your notes. Memory carries forward across sessions.

## Setup

1. Go to [get.mem.ai](https://get.mem.ai), sign up.
2. Download the Mac and iOS apps; they're where most of the value lives.
3. Free tier exists; paid is $14.99/mo for unlimited Mem X.
4. (Optional) Install the Chrome extension to "Mem It" any web page.
5. (Optional) Pipe email summaries into Mem via a forward address.

## How I use it day to day

* **Capture without ceremony.** Cmd+Shift+M anywhere on macOS opens a quick capture. I dump fragments — a half thought, a quote, a reference — and Mem files them.
* **Daily notes.** A continuous stream rather than a calendar of separate pages. Searching "what did I think about X last month" actually works.
* **Mem X for "what did I learn last week."** Surfaces fragments I'd forgotten across notes. Less useful for synthesis, more useful for recall.
* **Forwarded email.** Newsletter goes to my Mem inbox; Mem extracts the key insight and tags it. I rarely re read the original.
* **Cross note links** that the AI suggests. "This note is related to these three older ones." Sometimes useful, sometimes noise.

## Gotchas

* The "self organising" promise works best on hundreds of notes, less on tens. Early on it feels under powered.
* Export is fine (Markdown) but the network of links doesn't transfer cleanly. Plan to commit somewhat to staying in Mem.
* Mem X's Q&A quality is downstream of how much you write. Empty notes → empty answers.
* Mobile capture is great; mobile editing is fine; mobile search is occasionally laggy on large libraries.
* No native handwriting / sketching support. Apple Pencil users may prefer Notability or a hybrid setup.
