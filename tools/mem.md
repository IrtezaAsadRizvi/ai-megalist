# Mem: self-organizing notes with AI memory

Mem is an AI-native notes app in the second-brain category alongside [Notion AI](notion_ai.md), [Reflect](reflect.md), and [Obsidian](obsidian.md), pitched at people who hate folders. Mem is the notes app for people who hate folders. The premise is simple: you write things down, Mem indexes and connects them, and the AI surfaces the right note at the right time. The "self organising" pitch sounds gimmicky until you've used a tagged + foldered system long enough to resent it.

## What it actually is

A notes app (web, macOS, iOS) with AI baked in across the surface. Features include AI generated tags, semantic search, "Mem It" for sending content from anywhere, and Mem X - an assistant that drafts and answers questions from your notes. Memory carries forward across sessions.

## Setup

1. Go to [get.mem.ai](https://get.mem.ai), sign up.
2. Download the Mac and iOS apps; they're where most of the value lives.
3. Free tier exists; paid is $14.99/mo for unlimited Mem X.
4. (Optional) Install the Chrome extension to "Mem It" any web page.
5. (Optional) Pipe email summaries into Mem via a forward address.

## How I use it day to day

* **Capture without ceremony.** Cmd+Shift+M anywhere on macOS opens a quick capture. I dump fragments - a half thought, a quote, a reference - and Mem files them.
* **Daily notes.** A continuous stream rather than a calendar of separate pages. Searching "what did I think about X last month" actually works.
* **Mem X for "what did I learn last week."** Surfaces fragments I'd forgotten across notes. Less useful for synthesis, more useful for recall.
* **Forwarded email.** Newsletter goes to my Mem inbox; Mem extracts the key insight and tags it. I rarely re read the original.
* **Cross note links** that the AI suggests. "This note is related to these three older ones." Sometimes useful, sometimes noise.

## Gotchas

* The "self organising" promise works best on hundreds of notes, less on tens. Early on it feels under powered.
* Export is fine (Markdown) but the network of links doesn't transfer cleanly. Plan to commit somewhat to staying in Mem.
* Mem X's Q&A quality is downstream of how much you write. Empty notes → empty answers.
* Mobile capture is great; mobile editing is fine; mobile search is occasionally laggy on large libraries.
* No native handwriting / sketching support. Apple Pencil users may prefer Notability or a hybrid setup.

## Alternatives

* If you want a structured workspace with databases and pages, [Notion AI](notion_ai.md) is the bigger product.
* If you want OSS, local-first Markdown with AI as plugins, [Obsidian](obsidian.md) is the most flexible setup.
* If you want a writing-first single editor with networked notes, [Reflect](reflect.md) is the closer cousin.
* If you mostly need Q&A on docs rather than ongoing notes, [NotebookLM](notebooklm.md) is the focused tool.

## FAQ

### Is Mem free?

Yes, there's a free tier. Paid is $14.99/mo for unlimited Mem X (the AI assistant). The free tier is enough to evaluate; the AI value mostly arrives at the paid tier.

### Mem vs Notion AI - which should I use?

Mem when you want a continuous stream of fragments and AI-suggested links across them. [Notion AI](notion_ai.md) when you want structured pages, databases, and team collaboration. Different shapes - Mem is for one person's brain; Notion is for team workspaces.

### Does Mem work offline?

Limited - it's a cloud app first. For offline-first Markdown notes with local files you actually own, [Obsidian](obsidian.md) is the better fit.

### Can I export Mem notes?

Yes, Markdown export works. The network of AI-suggested links doesn't transfer cleanly though - plan to commit somewhat to staying in Mem if the link graph matters.
