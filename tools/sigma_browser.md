# Sigma AI Browser

SigmaOS is a macOS browser built around workspaces and AI assistants, not tabs. The pitch is that you organize your browsing into spaces (work, side project, research), and an AI named Airis lives in the sidebar to help with whatever's in front of you. It's a niche product, but for people who like tab obsessive browsers (former Arc users, in particular) it's the closest spiritual successor I've found.

## What it actually is

A macOS native web browser by SigmaOS Inc., built on Chromium, with a workspace based UI and a built in AI sidekick (Airis) that can summarize pages, answer questions about the current tab, and run multi step flows across open tabs. Free with a paid Pro tier for unlimited AI use and additional features.

## Setup

1. Download from [sigmaos.com](https://sigmaos.com) (macOS only).
2. Import bookmarks from your existing browser when prompted.
3. Create a couple of workspaces; the product makes more sense once you've split work from personal.
4. Open Airis from the right sidebar (cmd+J by default).
5. (Optional) Subscribe to Pro for unlimited AI prompts and workspace count.

## How I use it day to day

* **Workspace per project.** I keep one workspace pinned to a side project (docs, repo, deploy dashboard) so the open tabs reflect where I am. Closing the laptop and coming back puts me right back in context.
* **Airis on the active tab.** "Summarize this," "extract the table," "what does this error mean." Faster than copying into a separate chat tab.
* **Multi tab questions.** Asking Airis to compare information across two open tabs is the workflow that does most to justify the browser over a vanilla Chromium with a chat sidebar.

## Gotchas

* macOS only. If you switch between OSes, this is a non starter.
* Chromium based, so most extensions work, but a handful of niche ones break against the custom UI shell.
* The team is small; updates are steady but not fast. Some ex Arc users miss specific features.
* Privacy: Airis sends page content to a hosted backend. Read the privacy policy before pointing it at sensitive internal docs.
