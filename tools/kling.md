# Kling

Kling is the AI video tool to use when you'd rather generate ten variations than one perfect clip. The pricing is the lowest of the major models, the durations are the longest (up to two minutes per clip), and the quality on V3 is genuinely competitive with Runway and Veo on motion physics. For creators iterating heavily, it's the obvious value pick.

## What it actually is

Kuaishou's text and image to video model. Available globally through [klingai.com](https://www.klingai.com). Generates clips up to 120 seconds long at 1080p, with V3 (April 2026) the current top tier. Strong at human motion (lipsync, body movement) and at long duration coherence.

## Setup

1. Go to [klingai.com](https://www.klingai.com), sign up with email or Google.
2. Free tier offers 166 daily credits (~3 short videos/day).
3. Pricing: Standard $6.99/mo (660 credits/mo), Premium $25.99/mo (3000 credits), Master $69.99/mo (9000 credits + early access).
4. Click Image to Video or Text to Video, fill the prompt, set duration (5s, 10s, up to 120s on Master), generate.

## How I use it day to day

* **Image to video at scale.** I generate 5 to 10 motion variations from the same still and pick the best. Cheap iteration is the point.
* **Long form takes.** For a single ~30 second establishing shot, Kling reaches there with one generation; Runway and Veo need stitching.
* **Lipsync.** Kling's character lipsync (separate feature) is among the best, useful for AI presenter videos.
* **Motion Brush** for region specific motion control. Comparable to Runway's, lower quality but free.
* **Comparing on benchmarks.** I include Kling in any image to video A/B because the price floor makes it easy.

## Gotchas

* English prompts work; the model is trained on Chinese language data so style references with Chinese cultural context land harder than Western equivalents.
* Quality varies more than Veo. Some generations are Runway tier; others are noticeably weaker. Generate variations.
* The web app's UI is functional but less polished than competitors. The translations are sometimes awkward.
* Credits expire; the monthly quota doesn't roll over.
* Kling is owned by Kuaishou (a Chinese company). Some enterprises have data residency concerns; check if relevant.
