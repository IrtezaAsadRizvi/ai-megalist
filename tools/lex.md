# Lex: AI-native long-form writing editor

Lex sits in the writing & content category alongside [Sudowrite](sudowrite.md) and [Grammarly](grammarly.md), and the one I'd recommend to essayists and journalists who'd rather write than fight Google Docs. Lex is the AI native long form writing tool. Word and Google Docs added AI features; Lex was built around them. The result is an editor that gets out of your way for prose and steps in usefully for the things AI is genuinely good at - break out of a stuck paragraph, tighten a draft, suggest a counter argument. For essayists, journalists, and anyone whose primary output is words, Lex changes the writing rhythm.

## What it actually is

A web based long form editor at [lex.page](https://lex.page). Uses GPT‑4 / Claude under the hood; you can pick. Features include AI commands (`+++` to ask the AI to continue, ask a question, suggest improvements), AI critique (a panel that reviews your draft from a configurable POV), comment threads, draft history, and clean exports.

## Setup

1. Go to [lex.page](https://lex.page), sign up.
2. Free tier: limited monthly AI usage.
3. Pricing: Lex Pro $12/mo for higher AI limits, premium models, custom personas.
4. Start a new doc. Type. Use `+++` to summon AI commands.
5. (Optional) Custom AI personas - "Critic," "Editor," "Devil's Advocate" - for AI feedback in different stances.

## How I use it day to day

* **Drafting long pieces.** When I'm writing a 2000+ word essay, Lex's AI assist is unobtrusive and useful. Three keystrokes (`+++`) summon it; one keystroke dismisses it.
* **Break the writer's block.** Stuck mid paragraph; `+++ continue this paragraph for two more sentences in my voice.` Often the suggestion is wrong but it unsticks me.
* **AI critique.** Finished a draft; ask Lex's Critic persona for feedback. Tighter than a generic LLM prompt because the persona is preset.
* **Exporting clean.** Lex exports to Markdown, PDF, Word, plain text. None of the wrestling Google Docs imposes.
* **Drafts history.** Versioned; you can see what changed when. Useful for long pieces edited over weeks.

## Gotchas

* Word / Google Docs power features (templates, complex citations, footnotes) are minimal. Lex is for *writing*, not for *document production*.
* Collaboration features exist but are simpler than Google Docs'. For team writing with extensive review, Docs is still the workhorse.
* Free tier AI quota goes fast on long pieces. Pro is the floor for daily use.
* The AI's "voice match" depends on giving it enough of your writing as context. Short prompts produce generic completions.
* Mobile is web only; for serious mobile writing, consider iA Writer + ChatGPT instead.

## FAQ

### Is Lex free?

Yes - the free tier has limited monthly AI usage, enough to evaluate. Lex Pro is $12/mo and lifts the AI cap, adds premium models, and unlocks custom personas. Pro is the realistic floor for daily writing use.

### Lex vs Google Docs - which is better?

Different jobs. Google Docs wins on collaboration, templates, and document production (citations, footnotes, tracked changes). Lex wins on the writing experience itself - cleaner editor, AI built in via `+++`, painless exports.

### What does the `+++` shortcut do?

It summons Lex's AI - continue this paragraph, ask a question, suggest improvements. Three keystrokes to call it, one keystroke to dismiss. The hotkey is the design and it's why writing in Lex feels lighter than wrestling AI into Google Docs.

### Can Lex match my writing voice?

Partially - it depends on giving it enough of your own writing as context. Short prompts produce generic completions. The AI critique personas (Critic, Editor, Devil's Advocate) work better than freeform "rewrite this" because the stance is preset.

### Does Lex export to Markdown?

Yes - Markdown, PDF, Word, and plain text. Cleaner than the export wrestling Google Docs imposes, which is one of the underrated reasons Lex is pleasant for technical writers.
