# Brisk Teaching: AI Chrome extension built for K-12 teachers

Brisk Teaching sits in the education cluster alongside [Khanmigo](khanmigo.md), [NotebookLM](notebooklm.md), and [Quizlet AI](quizlet.md), but unique in being teacher-facing rather than student-facing. Brisk Teaching is the AI extension built specifically for K 12 teachers, sitting inside Google Docs, Slides, and Drive. It does what the general purpose AI tools do (summarize, generate, give feedback) but framed and scoped for classroom workflows: leveling reading passages, generating quizzes, giving rubric aligned feedback on student writing. It's a verticalized AI product, and the vertical fit is the value.

## What it actually is

A Chrome extension and web app for teachers, by Brisk Teaching Inc. Integrates with Google Workspace (Docs, Slides, Forms) and a growing list of LMS platforms. Free tier; paid tiers for power users and schools.

## Setup

1. Install the Brisk extension from the Chrome Web Store.
2. Sign in with a Google account (school or personal).
3. Open Google Docs, Slides, or Forms; the Brisk sidebar appears on demand.
4. Pick an action: change reading level, generate quiz, give feedback, write an IEP, etc.
5. (Optional) Subscribe for higher generation limits and additional features.

## How I use it day to day

I'm not a teacher; the educators I've talked to who use it report:

* **Reading level adjustment.** Take a passage; rewrite at a 6th grade level (or 3rd, or 9th). Useful for differentiated instruction without manual rewrites.
* **Quiz generation.** Source a Google Doc; produce a quiz aligned to it. Saves a lot of busywork.
* **Feedback on student writing.** Brisk gives rubric aligned comments; teachers review and tweak before sharing.
* **Lesson plan drafts.** Frames the prompt right for the K 12 audience; better than asking ChatGPT cold.

For teachers, the value is having a tool that already understands the workflow. Calling Claude directly is more flexible but requires building the prompt engineering yourself.

## Gotchas

* Output requires teacher review. AI generated quizzes can have wrong answers, generated feedback can miss context.
* Privacy and student data: read the policy carefully if you're using it on student work. School level deployment usually addresses this; individual teacher use less so.
* Free tier limits are generous but the more useful flows tend to be on paid plans.
* The product is K 12 oriented; higher ed tools (Cohort or Khanmigo for tutoring) fit different audiences.

## Alternatives

* If you want the student-facing K-12 tutor side rather than teacher tools, [Khanmigo](khanmigo.md) is the Khan Academy answer.
* If you want to turn course material into student Q&A and audio overviews, [NotebookLM](notebooklm.md) is what students will actually open.
* If you want flashcards and Q-Chat tutoring on the study side, [Quizlet AI](quizlet.md) covers that bench.
* If you want general-purpose AI without the K-12 framing, [Claude](claude.md) or [ChatGPT](chatgpt.md) are more flexible at the cost of doing the prompt work yourself.

## FAQ

### Is Brisk Teaching free?

Yes - free tier covers a generous chunk of generations. The more useful flows tend to live on the paid individual and school plans.

### Brisk Teaching vs ChatGPT for lesson planning?

Brisk wins on workflow fit - prompts are framed for the K-12 audience, sidebar lives inside Google Docs / Slides / Forms. [ChatGPT](chatgpt.md) is more flexible but you build the prompt engineering yourself. Most teachers I've talked to prefer the verticalized tool.

### Is Brisk Teaching safe for student data?

Read the privacy policy carefully if you'll use it on student work. School-level deployments usually address this; individual teacher use less so.

### What can Brisk Teaching actually do?

Reading-level adjustment, quiz generation, rubric-aligned feedback on student writing, IEP drafting, lesson plan drafts. Output requires teacher review - AI-generated quizzes can have wrong answers.
