# Reclaim

Reclaim is the calendar tool that defends your focus time without the heavyweight project management Motion adds. Pure auto scheduling: tell Reclaim what you want done; it finds time on your calendar; it adapts when meetings shift. For knowledge workers who already use a separate task manager, Reclaim is the lighter complement.

## What it actually is

A web + mobile app that connects to Google Calendar (Outlook in beta) and adds smart auto scheduling. Features include Habits (recurring focus blocks), Tasks (one off work), Smart 1:1s (auto find time with another person), and Calendar Defense (auto reschedule when conflicts appear).

## Setup

1. Go to [reclaim.ai](https://reclaim.ai), sign up with Google or Microsoft.
2. Free tier: limited features, decent for evaluation.
3. Pricing: Lite $8/mo, Business $13/mo, Enterprise $19/mo.
4. Connect calendar(s). Reclaim treats existing meetings as fixed.
5. Set up Habits ("daily 30 min planning at 9 AM"), Tasks ("write the Q2 doc, 4 hours, due Friday"), and Smart 1:1s.
6. Reclaim schedules around your existing meetings and re schedules when conflicts emerge.

## How I use it day to day

* **Habits for routine work.** Daily exercise block at 7 AM, weekly review on Friday afternoon, deep work sessions Tuesday and Thursday morning. Reclaim places them flexibly within constraints I set.
* **Tasks with deadlines.** Drop a task in; Reclaim picks the time. Less ceremony than Motion's full PM layer.
* **Smart 1:1s.** "Schedule a 30 min with Sara every other Tuesday." Reclaim finds time on both our calendars; reschedules if either changes.
* **Calendar Defense.** When someone books over my focus block, Reclaim moves the focus block automatically and books a free spot.
* **Slack status sync.** Reclaim updates my Slack status based on what's on the calendar (in a meeting, in focus time). Subtle but useful.

## Gotchas

* Reclaim manipulates your real calendar. The first week, watch what it does and override when it gets it wrong; trust builds with verification.
* Cross calendar conflicts (work + personal) need both calendars connected with proper visibility.
* Pricing is tier gated by features. Some habits are limited on Lite; Business is the floor for serious daily use.
* Doesn't handle dependencies between tasks (this requires that). For dependency aware scheduling, Motion is more capable.
* Mobile app is functional but the desktop is where most users live.
