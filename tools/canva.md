# Canva: AI design suite for non-designers and small teams

Canva sits in the design cluster alongside [Adobe Firefly](adobe_firefly.md), [Gamma](gamma.md), and Figma, but aimed squarely at non-designers who need a flyer or a social post in five minutes. Canva is the design tool that 250 million monthly users actually open, and the AI features (Magic Studio) ship at the rate of a tool with that much surface area to fund. If you're not a designer but you need a flyer, an Instagram post, a logo, a video edit - Canva probably has a template, and now an AI button next to it.

## What it actually is

A consumer / SMB design platform. Magic Studio bundles dozens of AI features: Magic Design (prompt → designed asset), Magic Write (copy generation), Magic Edit (inpaint a photo), Magic Switch (resize across formats), Magic Animate (still → motion), Magic Media (image and video gen). Web, iOS, Android.

## Setup

1. Go to [canva.com](https://www.canva.com), sign up.
2. Free tier covers the basics + limited Magic Studio.
3. Pricing: Pro $14.99/mo (1 user), Teams $30/mo (3 users), Enterprise.
4. Pick a template (or start blank); the AI features sit in the toolbar and the right panel.
5. (For brand work) set up Brand Kit: logos, colors, fonts. Magic Design will respect them.

## How I use it day to day

* **Honest:** Canva isn't my main design tool, but I keep a Pro account because it solves "I need a thing in 5 minutes" for non design tasks.
* **Magic Design.** Type "Instagram carousel about three productivity tips, modern dark theme." Output is decent and ready to publish.
* **Magic Switch.** I made a slide for a presentation; switch to Instagram square, LinkedIn banner, story format - Canva resizes intelligently. Saves an hour vs manual.
* **Background removal and Magic Edit.** Faster than firing up Photoshop for one off photo cleanup.
* **Brand Kit.** Once I set my brand colors and fonts, every Magic Design respects them. Maintains consistency without a designer in the loop.
* **For non visual people on my team.** Canva is the floor; people who'd otherwise produce ugly slides produce passable ones.

## Gotchas

* The output quality is bounded by templates and AI. For premium brand work, use Figma or Adobe; Canva is the right floor not the right ceiling.
* Some AI features (Magic Media, especially video) burn credits fast. Track usage on Pro.
* Exporting at high resolution requires Pro. Free tier exports cap at 1080p.
* Editor performance can chug on complex multi page documents. Break into smaller files.
* The asset library mixes free, Pro only, and "subject to extra charge." Watch the icons before exporting.

## Alternatives

* If you need real product or UI design with handoff to engineering, [Figma](figma.md) is the right tool, not Canva.
* If you need a prompt-to-deck experience specifically, [Gamma](gamma.md) is more focused.
* If you need commercially-safe AI image generation inside a Creative Cloud workflow, [Adobe Firefly](adobe_firefly.md) is the licensing-grade alternative.
* If you want brand-consistent vector + raster as the focus, [Recraft](recraft.md) leans that way.

## FAQ

### Is Canva free?

Yes - the free tier covers basics plus limited Magic Studio. Pro is $14.99/mo (1 user), Teams $30/mo (3 users). High-resolution exports require Pro.

### Canva vs Figma - which one?

Different jobs. Canva is for templated marketing/social design and one-off graphics. [Figma](figma.md) is for UI design with components, variants, dev handoff. Don't pick Canva for product UI; don't pick Figma for an Instagram carousel.

### What is Magic Studio?

Canva's bundle of AI features: Magic Design (prompt to designed asset), Magic Write (copy), Magic Edit (inpaint photos), Magic Switch (resize across formats), Magic Animate, Magic Media. Some chew through credits fast on Pro - track usage.

### Are Canva's AI images safe to use commercially?

Pro tier grants commercial usage for most AI features. Free tier is more restricted. Read the current terms before commercial use - they shift.

### Canva vs Gamma for slides?

Gamma is more polished for AI-generated decks specifically; Canva is broader (slides plus social plus print plus video). I use Gamma for first drafts of decks and Canva for everything else templated.
